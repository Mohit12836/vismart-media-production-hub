# Emotion-to-direction rubric
Emotion is not an effect preset. Analyze (A) speaker intention, (B) literal proposition, (C) viewer target state, (D) rhythm/prosody, (E) contextual sensitivity.

Per beat record: `primary`, `secondary`, `intensity` from 0 to 1, `delivery` (pace/pitch/volume/pauses), `confidence` (human-verified, audio-informed, text-inferred), `visual_rationale`.

| Emotion | Visual possibility | Audio option | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| curiosity | partial reveal, negative space, detail framing | restrained rise | 8 rapid jumps with no payoff |
| concern | situational wide shot then meaningful reaction | quiet ambience / pause | melodramatic red alarm for ordinary problem |
| suspense | occlusion, held frame, slow perspective shift | sparse texture, silence | constant loud hits |
| relief | wider framing, brighter but context-sensitive lighting | resolved harmony | hard brightness cut on every relief word |
| urgency | clear stakes and quick functional cuts | rhythmic accents | extreme speed that hides facts |
| wonder | scale contrast, world reveal | soft swell | gratuitous lens flare |
| humor | setup-hold-payoff, reaction framing | carefully placed sting | sound effects before joke lands |
| authority | stable composition, sourced visual evidence | clean voice | fake numbers and fake testimonials |

Human override always wins. Emotion values do not prove actual vocal prosody. User-provided recording can be analyzed separately; explain confidence and limitations.

## Emotional QA questions
1. What is being said, literally and implicitly?
2. What should the audience know/feel after this beat?
3. Which single visual action carries that meaning?
4. What can be removed? Is silence stronger?
5. Is the intensity appropriate to the stakes and genre?
